Why we write these

Coastal Connecticut is hard on outdoor construction

Salt air, clay pockets, ledge two feet down, freeze-thaw cycles every February. We build for that reality instead of hoping around it, and these notes pass on what the job site has taught us — the parts nobody sees that decide whether the parts you see stay put.

Hardscape

Bluestone vs. Pavers: What Actually Holds Up in Coastal CT

Salt air, freeze-thaw and shade moss all change the answer. Here’s how we choose patio material for Norwalk properties — and where each one disappoints.

  • We set bluestone, granite, clay brick and premium pavers on an 8–12" compacted base with polymeric joints.
  • Most Norwalk stone patios land between $28 and $55 per square foot, depending on the stone, base depth, grading and access.
  • The price is fixed in writing after a site visit — no hourly surprises.

Masonry

Why Retaining Walls Fail (It’s Almost Never the Stone)

Nine out of ten failed walls we rebuild had no drainage stone, no filter fabric and no weep outlet. A look behind the face of a wall that lasts.

  • Dry-laid fieldstone, mortared veneer and engineered segmental walls all need proper drainage behind them.
  • In most Norwalk neighborhoods, walls over four feet, work in wetlands buffers, or structures near a property line require permits. We handle the applications and engineering when needed.
  • Repointing, resetting settled stone and fixing the drainage that caused the problem are often a fraction of a full rebuild.

Planting

12 Plants That Thrive in Fairfield County Front Yards

Deer-resistant, salt-tolerant and forgiving of clay. Our go-to list for beds that still look intentional in February.

  • We favor native and coastal-tolerant plantings chosen for Connecticut soil and shade, laid out to look established from the first season.
  • Fall is the ideal season to plant and prepare for a better-looking yard next year.
  • Foundation plantings, privacy screening, perennial gardens and tree and shrub installs all start from the same site walk.

Drainage

Standing Water After Every Storm? Start Here

Before you install a drain, find out where the water comes from. A simple three-step diagnosis you can do with a hose and a level.

  • Drainage is half of what we do: French drains, dry wells, regrading, downspout extensions and permeable paving all get water moving away from the house.
  • Catch basins and downspout tie-ins push water away from your foundation instead of toward it.

Lighting

Landscape Lighting Mistakes That Make a Yard Look Cheap

Even spacing, cool white bulbs and lights aimed at nothing. What to do instead so the stonework carries the night.

  • We install low-voltage LED uplighting, path lights and wall washing, and set them at dusk with you on site.
  • Timers and zones keep the walks safe and the stonework showing after dark.

Seasonal

Spring Checklist for Your Patio, Walls and Beds

Ten minutes of inspection in April prevents a rebuild in October. What to look for after a Connecticut winter.

  • Settled stone, open joints and walls that have started to lean are all repairable before they become rebuilds.
  • A spring cleanup covers leaf and debris removal, full lawn cleanup, bed edging, shrub and hedge trimming, and fresh mulch put down properly.

Timing your project

When to build, when to plant, when to plan

The calendar matters as much as the materials in Connecticut.

Masonry: April through November

That’s the ideal window, but we build well into December using heated enclosures and cold-weather mortar.

Planting: think fall

Fall is the ideal season to plant new beds and greenery so the yard is ready to go next year.

Design: use the winter

Winter is the smartest time to design and get on the spring schedule before the calendar fills.

Quick answers

What homeowners ask us while planning

Ask about your yard →

How long does a typical project take?

A walkway is usually 2–4 days, a patio 1–2 weeks, and a full backyard with walls, lighting and plantings 3–5 weeks. Your proposal includes a realistic start and finish window.

Can you fix a soggy yard or water in the basement?

Yes. French drains, dry wells, regrading, downspout extensions and permeable paving all get your water moving away from the house.

Do you repair existing patios, walls and steps?

Yes — repointing, resetting settled stone, rebuilding failed walls, and fixing the drainage that caused the problem in the first place.

What kind of warranty do you provide?

Two years on workmanship for hardscape and masonry, one full season on plantings we install and maintain, plus the manufacturer’s warranty on pavers and lighting.
