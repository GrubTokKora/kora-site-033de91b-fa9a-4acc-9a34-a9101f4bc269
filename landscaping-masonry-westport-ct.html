What building in Westport involves

Salt air near the Sound

Homes near Compo Beach, Saugatuck Shores and along the Saugatuck River get salt spray in the wind and salt on the walks every winter. It’s hard on some stone, mortar and plants. We pick materials and plants with that exposure in mind, and we set patios on a deep compacted base so freeze-thaw doesn’t heave them.

Wet clay and ledge inland

Away from the shore, much of Westport sits on the same mix of clay, glacial rock and shallow ledge found across Fairfield County. Clay holds water, which is why so many slopes and walls here start to move. Every wall we build gets drainage stone, filter fabric and an outlet behind it.

Slopes and old timber walls

Plenty of Westport properties have a grade change held back by timber walls that are now rotting out. Our Westport project below is a typical case: an eroding slope beside the house, replaced with tiered fieldstone walls built to drain properly.

Approvals near water

Work near the shoreline, wetlands or a watercourse can need review before it starts, and taller walls need a building permit. We’ll tell you on the site walk if your project is likely to need one, and we handle the paperwork for walls that do.

Do you work in Westport, CT?

Yes. Westport is minutes from our yard, so we’re regularly on site in Westport for walls, patios, drainage, planting and seasonal maintenance. Site walks and estimates are free.

Which patio stone holds up best near the water in Westport?

Both natural bluestone and quality concrete pavers can last near the Sound if the base is right. Salt spray and de-icing salt are harder on some surfaces than others, and shade and moss change how slippery a stone gets. We go through the trade-offs in Bluestone vs. Pavers in Coastal CT, and on site we’ll recommend what suits your exposure.

Will my Westport project need town approval?

Sometimes. Retaining walls over about four feet usually need a building permit and an engineered design. Work close to the shoreline, a wetland or a watercourse can bring in coastal or wetlands review, and properties in a historic district have their own rules. We’ll flag what applies on the site walk, and we handle permit applications and engineering when a wall needs them.
